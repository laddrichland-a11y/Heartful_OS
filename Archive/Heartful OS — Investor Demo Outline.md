# Heartful OS — Investor Demo
**Recorded walkthrough · target run time 10:00 · beat sheet**

---

## Before you hit record

- Log in ahead of time. Never record a login screen.
- Have one **hero client** seeded with a full history — sessions across several phases, a couple of AI summaries, messages, a completed intake form. The History tab is your proof of depth; it only lands if it's populated.
- Open a second browser window logged into the **client portal** as that same client. Switching windows is faster and cleaner than logging out mid-demo.
- Close Slack, email, and notifications. Hide your bookmarks bar.
- Zoom the browser to ~110% so text is readable in a compressed video.
- Decide now: you will not fix anything live. If something breaks, keep talking and move on.

---

## 0:00 – 1:00 · The problem (talking head or title card)

The only minute where you're not on screen sharing. Set the stakes before any UI appears.

- Name the category in one sentence: practice management software built for psychedelic-assisted therapy and guided journey work.
- The problem: this modality doesn't fit conventional EHR software. A journey isn't a series of interchangeable 50-minute appointments — it's a **structured arc** with distinct phases, each with its own prep, paperwork, and risk profile.
- What practitioners do today: a patchwork of Google Docs, calendar reminders, text messages, and paper intake forms. Every practitioner rebuilds the same system from scratch.
- Land the thesis: **the journey has a shape, and software should know it.**

*Don't say "AI" yet. Earn it.*

---

## 1:00 – 2:00 · The model, not the app

Still no clicking. Put up a simple graphic of the six phases if you have one.

- Walk the arc: **Intake → Preparation → Journey Day → 12-hour Check-In → Integration 1 → Integration 2.**
- Make the point that this is the product's real intellectual property: the journey model is encoded in the data layer, not just the UI. Status transitions, session types, and phase workspaces all derive from it.
- Why that matters commercially: it's the thing a generic EHR can't retrofit, and it's what makes the AI useful rather than decorative.

---

## 2:00 – 3:30 · Dashboard — the practitioner's morning

Screen share begins here.

- Open the dashboard. Frame it as: *this is what a practitioner sees with their coffee.*
- Point at active clients, upcoming sessions, outstanding forms, unread messages.
- The story to tell: no hunting. The work surfaces itself.
- **Don't click a session from the dashboard during the demo** — that link currently lands on the client's Documents tab rather than the session. Navigate via the Clients list instead.
- Move to the Clients list briefly, then into your hero client.

---

## 3:30 – 5:30 · The client record — your strongest two minutes

This is the heart of the demo. Slow down here; don't rush to the AI.

- **Header:** name, status, payment summary, portal status, emergency contact. One screen, no hunting.
- **Milestone progress bar and phase pills:** *this client is in Integration 1 — you can see it at a glance, and so can they.* Click one phase pill to show that each phase has a dedicated workspace.
- **Action Card:** the smart tile row. Emphasize that it shows only what's currently relevant and disappears entirely when nothing is pending. Investors respond to restraint — a screen that empties itself is a design decision, not an absence.
- **Documents tab:** forms grouped by journey stage. Note that this mirrors how a practitioner actually works a case.
- **History tab:** finish this section here. Scroll the full audit trail — every session, form, message, task, check-in, milestone. Say it plainly: *this is the clinical record, the compliance story, and the training data, all at once.*

---

## 5:30 – 7:00 · AI Copilot — the payoff

Now you've earned it.

- Open a session and hit **Prepare Me**. Let it generate on camera if it's reliably fast; if it's ever slow, have a pre-generated briefing ready to open instead. Dead air kills a demo.
- Read one or two lines of the briefing out loud. Specific beats impressive — pick a line that clearly draws on this client's actual history.
- Make the differentiating argument explicitly: **this isn't a chatbot bolted onto a CRM.** The briefing is good because the system understands the phase, the session type, and the full history. Generic AI can't produce it because generic software doesn't have the structure underneath.
- Show the **Living Journey Summary** in Journey & AI as the second beat — the longitudinal view.
- Mention the hide/finish controls briefly as evidence you've built for real workflow, not just for a demo.

*Naming note: you have three surfaces called some version of "AI Copilot." In a recorded demo, refer to them by function — "the prep tool," "the saved insights" — and don't put the sidebar and the client tab on screen back to back.*

---

## 7:00 – 8:30 · The client portal — the second user

Switch to the other browser window.

- Reframe: *every practice management tool serves the practitioner. This one also serves the client.*
- Show the portal Home: journey progress bar and action items. The client sees the same arc the practitioner sees.
- Forms & Check-Ins: forms are filled here, not on paper. The 12-hour check-in is the one to highlight — it's the moment of highest clinical value and the one most likely to be missed without a system.
- Messages: one thread, both sides.
- The strategic point for this audience: **two-sided engagement.** The client logging in is a retention mechanism and a data source, not just a convenience.

---

## 8:30 – 9:30 · Where it goes

Back to talking head, or a simple roadmap slide.

- Be candid about one or two gaps and what's next — portal assignments (practitioners can message but not yet assign homework from the app), and the 48-hour reflection check-in already modeled in the data layer. Naming real gaps buys credibility for everything you claimed earlier.
- Sketch the expansion: multi-practitioner practices, outcome reporting across a caseload, and the aggregate dataset the History tab is quietly building.
- One line on the moat: the journey model plus the accumulated structured history is what compounds.

---

## 9:30 – 10:00 · Close

- Restate the thesis in one sentence: the journey has a shape, and Heartful OS is built around it.
- One clear ask — a call, a pilot practice, an intro to a clinic.
- Stop talking. End the recording cleanly rather than trailing off.

---

## Pacing guardrails

| Section | Budget | Cut first if long |
|---|---|---|
| Problem + model | 2:00 | Trim the model to 40s |
| Dashboard | 1:30 | Cut to 45s — it's setup, not payoff |
| Client record | 2:00 | Protect this |
| AI Copilot | 1:30 | Protect this |
| Client portal | 1:30 | Drop Messages |
| Roadmap + close | 1:30 | Trim roadmap detail |

Your two protected sections are the client record and the AI briefing. If you're running long at the 6:00 mark, shorten the portal walkthrough rather than rushing the close — a hurried ask reads as a weak one.

**Record it twice.** The second take is always 90 seconds shorter and noticeably better.
